## Source Text

尘劳十二载，今又逢中秋。

烟云吞婵娟，岁月煎人寿。

半生未成名，少年怯回首。

当时夜色好，不觉风凉透。

## AI Translation

*Please translate my creation into an English poem.*

* Twelve years worn down by dust and toil, once more I meet the Mid-Autumn moon.

* Mist and clouds devour the moon so bright, while passing years wear down this mortal life.

* Half a lifetime passed, yet no fame earned, I dare not turn and face my younger years.

* The evening light back then seemed so fair, not knowing how the chilling wind cut through.

>This poem "Mid-Autumn Night: Expressing My Heart" in the five-character ancient style is a compact yet deeply moving meditation on time, failure, and the quiet sorrow of middle age, set against the backdrop of the Mid-Autumn Festival.
>
>The opening line grounds us in weariness. "Twelve years of dust and toil" is not a complaint but a fact — the kind of statement one makes when exhaustion has become so habitual it needs no elaboration. "Once more I meet" carries a faint bitterness: the festival returns every year, and every year he is still here, still alone.
>
>The second couplet fuses sky and soul. "Clouds swallow the bright moon" is both weather and fate — beauty and hope obscured by forces beyond control. "Years simmer away one's life" uses a culinary metaphor for slow, irreversible erosion. The two images press together like two hands closing on a throat.
>
>The third couplet is the rawest. "Half a life, yet no fame" — no excuse, no blame, just the plain truth. Then "I fear to look back at my youth" — the word "fear" (怯) is crucial. He does not refuse to remember; he is afraid of what he will see. The contrast between who he was and who he is has become unbearable.
>
>The final couplet is where the poem reaches its true depth. "Back then the night was beautiful" recalls a young man full of fire and ambition. He did not feel the wind because his heart was burning. "Not knowing the wind had pierced through" is not about ignorance of temperature — it is about a man who once could not be chilled, now pierced to the bone. The cold was always there. What changed was him. The poem ends on this devastating realization: the tragedy is not that the world grew harsh, but that he grew vulnerable.
>
>In forty characters, the poem traces an arc from weariness to shame to a quiet, irreversible loss of inner fire. Its power lies in what it withholds — there is no crying, no cursing, only a man standing under a clouded moon, finally feeling what he once could not.
